What it does

What the journey works out for you

The schemes, on your numbers

The 5% Deposit Scheme, Help to Buy, a 10% deposit with LMI and saving to 20% sit side by side: when you could buy, the cash you need, the LMI and the repayment. The price and income caps are checked for your state.

A maximum price

Stamp duty for your state with any first home concession applied, legal costs, and your repayment tested the way lenders test it, with a 3% buffer on the rate. You finish with one number to take to an inspection or an auction.

Your contract deadlines

Enter the dates from your contract and each one is counted down: cooling-off, building and pest, finance approval, settlement. You can add them to your phone calendar.

Suburbs with dated figures

Sale medians for Victoria and metro Adelaide, and median rents for Queensland, South Australia and Tasmania, from state government data. Each figure shows its period and source. NSW, WA, the ACT and the NT are not covered yet.

A record of every place

Log each home you browse, inspect or bid on, with the asking price and a note. Each one is compared with your maximum price.

Free, and shareable

No signup to start. Your progress saves in your browser. A free account keeps it on all your devices and lets a partner follow along or edit with you.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

Rates and rules

Stamp duty schedules and first home buyer concessions come from each state revenue office. The 5% Deposit Scheme and Help to Buy settings come from Housing Australia, tax rates from the ATO, and the 3% serviceability buffer from APRA.

The figures in use are the FY2026-27 settings. I check them against the official sources at the start of each financial year, and the calculators use the same formulas as the journey.

Suburb figures

Suburb rents and sale medians come from state government open data, and each figure is shown with its period and its source. Populations come from the ABS 2021 Census.

Where a state does not publish a suburb-level figure, the page leaves it out. There are no investment scores and no growth forecasts on this site.

The methodology page lists the formulas and what is measured, and the data sources page lists each dataset, its licence and its period. I built EquitySight while buying my own first home, and I am not a financial adviser. More about who runs it.

Common questions

First home buyer questions

How much deposit do I need to buy a home in Australia?

A 20% deposit avoids Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI). First home buyers can buy with a 5% deposit and no LMI under the federal 5% Deposit Scheme (formerly the First Home Guarantee). Without a scheme, a 10% deposit usually means LMI is added to the loan. On a $650,000 home, 5% is $32,500 and 20% is $130,000. Stamp duty, conveyancing and inspections come on top. The deposit calculator works it out for your price and state.

What stamp duty do first home buyers pay in NSW, VIC or QLD?

Each state sets its own transfer duty and its own first home buyer relief. In NSW first home buyers pay no duty up to $800,000, with a concession up to $1,000,000. In Victoria the exemption runs to $600,000, with a concession up to $750,000. In Queensland first home buyers pay no duty on established homes up to $700,000 (phasing out by $800,000) and none on new homes at any price. These are the FY2026-27 settings. The stamp duty calculator covers all 8 states and territories.

How much can an Australian bank lend me?

Lenders start with your after-tax income, subtract your living expenses (the higher of what you declare or the Household Expenditure Measure benchmark) and your existing debt repayments, then test whether what is left covers the new loan at your interest rate plus a 3% buffer. Credit card limits count as a debt even with nothing owing. The borrowing power calculator runs that test on your numbers.

What is the APRA 3% serviceability buffer?

APRA, the banking regulator, expects lenders to check that a borrower could still make repayments if their interest rate were 3 percentage points higher. If your rate is 6.25%, the bank tests your repayments at 9.25%. The buffer has been 3% since October 2021, and it is the main reason banks lend less than borrowers expect.

What is the difference between LMI and the First Home Owner Grant?

Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI) is a cost. The lender takes it out when your deposit is under 20% and you pay the premium, usually by adding it to the loan. The First Home Owner Grant (FHOG) is a payment from your state government to first home buyers who buy or build a new home. For FY2026-27 it is $10,000 in NSW, Victoria and WA, $15,000 in SA, $20,000 in Tasmania, $30,000 in Queensland and $50,000 in the NT. The ACT has no grant. The grants calculator checks the price caps and conditions for your state.

Where do the numbers on EquitySight come from?

Stamp duty schedules come from each state revenue office, tax rates from the ATO, scheme settings from Housing Australia, the serviceability buffer from APRA, the cash rate from the RBA, suburb populations from the ABS 2021 Census, and suburb rents and sale medians from state government open data. The methodology and data sources pages list each source and its period.
