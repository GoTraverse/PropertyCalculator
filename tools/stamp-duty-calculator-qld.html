Queensland transfer duty rates

Queensland's stamp duty is called transfer duty in the Duties Act 2001 (Qld). The Queensland Revenue Office (QRO) charges it on the dutiable value, which is normally the purchase price. QRO can use a higher market value if it thinks the contract price has been understated.

Investors pay the standard rates:

Dutiable valueStandard duty
Up to $5,000Nil
$5,000 to $75,0001.5% over $5,000
$75,000 to $540,000$1,050 + 3.5% over $75,000
$540,000 to $1,000,000$17,325 + 4.5% over $540,000
Above $1,000,000$38,025 + 5.75% over $1,000,000

Home concession

If you'll live in the property as your principal place of residence, you pay the lower home concession rate instead. It isn't limited to first home buyers, and it saves up to $7,175 against the standard rate. You have to move in within 12 months of settlement, live there continuously for at least 12 months, and claim the concession when the transfer is stamped or before. Sell, lease or move out within that period and QRO can claw it back. Holiday homes and investment properties pay the standard rate.

First home concession

A first home buyer gets a second concession on top of the home concession. On an established home:

  • Up to $700,000: no duty.
  • $700,000 to $800,000: QRO takes a fixed amount for each $10,000 price band off the home concession duty, starting at $17,350 and stepping down to nil at $800,000. A $730,000 first home pays $6,555.
  • Above $800,000: home concession duty only.

For contracts signed from 1 May 2025, a first home buyer pays no duty at any price on a brand-new home or on residential vacant land. The old $350,000 and $500,000 caps on land are gone. Duty applies only to any non-residential part of the land.

To qualify you must be 18 or older, never have owned residential property anywhere in the world, and move in within 12 months of settlement. From 1 August 2026 you also need to be an Australian citizen, a permanent resident or a specified foreign retiree to get any Queensland home concession.

The $30,000 First Home Owner Grant for new homes is a cash grant, also run by QRO, and doesn't change the duty. It was legislated in June 2026 to continue for contracts signed from 1 July 2026.

What the calculator leaves out

It treats every dwelling as an established home. A first home buyer on a brand-new home pays no duty at any price, so ignore any figure it shows you above $700,000. It doesn't check the 1 August 2026 citizenship and residency rule, and it can't split off a non-residential part of a block.
