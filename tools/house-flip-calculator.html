How the profit is worked out

  • Total cost (in) = purchase price + stamp duty + buying conveyancing + inspections + renovation budget + renovation contingency + (monthly holding cost × number of months).
  • Net proceeds (out) = expected sale price − agent commission − selling conveyancing − styling/staging. Agent commission is sale price × commission rate.
  • Net profit (before tax) = net proceeds − total cost.
  • ROI = net profit ÷ total cost, as a percentage.
  • Annualised return = ROI × (12 ÷ months held), so flips of different lengths can be compared.

The contingency is 15% of the renovation budget, added automatically for problems that only show up once walls are opened (wiring, plumbing, asbestos, termite damage). Stamp duty isn't calculated here. Get the figure from the stamp duty calculator and enter it.

Worked example

  • Purchase price: $520,000
  • Stamp duty: $18,000 (varies by state and whether concessions apply)
  • Buying conveyancing and inspections: $2,300 combined
  • Renovation budget: $85,000, plus a 15% contingency of $12,750
  • Holding period: 8 months at $3,200 per month = $25,600 (loan interest, council rates, insurance, utilities)
  • Expected sale price: $750,000
  • Agent commission: 2.2% = $16,500; selling conveyancing $1,500; styling $3,000

Total cost in is $520,000 + $18,000 + $2,300 + $85,000 + $12,750 + $25,600 = $663,650. Net proceeds out are $750,000 − $16,500 − $1,500 − $3,000 = $729,000. Net profit before tax is $65,350, an ROI of 9.8%, which annualises to 14.8% over the eight-month hold.

The property was held under 12 months, so the whole gain is added to taxable income. At a 37% marginal rate that leaves around $41,000 after tax.

Tax on a flip

Under ATO rules, profit on an investment property is normally a capital gain. An individual who holds the property for more than 12 months generally gets the 50% CGT discount, which halves the taxable gain. A flip sold inside 12 months misses the discount, so the whole gain is added to assessable income and taxed at the marginal rate. The capital gains tax calculator works out the tax.

If the ATO decides you're running a property-flipping business or a profit-making scheme, the profit may be treated as ordinary income, with no CGT discount, and GST may apply to the sale. The line between investor and trader depends on the facts and your intention. The main-residence exemption can apply if you live in the property, but the ATO looks closely at people who move in only to claim it while flipping as a business.

What this calculator leaves out

  • Capital gains tax, income tax and GST.
  • Finance costs other than the monthly holding cost: loan establishment fees, lenders mortgage insurance (LMI, which typically applies above 80% LVR), break costs and capitalised interest. Add them to your inputs if they apply.
  • Subdivision, multi-dwelling development and rent earned during the hold. It models one purchase and one sale.
