A before-tax estimate. It leaves out capital gains tax, income tax on the profit, GST (for developers) and finance costs beyond the holding period entered.

How the profit is worked out

  • Total cost (in) = purchase price + stamp duty + buying conveyancing + inspections + renovation budget + renovation contingency + (monthly holding cost × number of months).
  • Net proceeds (out) = expected sale price − agent commission − selling conveyancing − styling/staging. Agent commission is sale price × commission rate.
  • Net profit (before tax) = net proceeds − total cost.
  • ROI = net profit ÷ total cost, as a percentage.
  • Annualised return = ROI × (12 ÷ months held), so flips of different lengths can be compared.

The contingency field adds 15% on top of your renovation budget, to allow for the hidden problems that turn up once walls are opened (wiring, plumbing, asbestos, termite damage). Stamp duty isn't calculated here. Get the figure from the stamp duty calculator and enter it.

Worked example

  • Purchase price: $520,000
  • Stamp duty: $18,000 (varies by state and whether concessions apply)
  • Buying conveyancing and inspections: $2,300 combined
  • Renovation budget: $85,000, plus a 15% contingency of $12,750
  • Holding period: 8 months at $3,200 per month = $25,600 (loan interest, council rates, insurance, utilities)
  • Expected sale price: $750,000
  • Agent commission: 2.2% = $16,500; selling conveyancing $1,500; styling $3,000

Total cost in is $520,000 + $18,000 + $2,300 + $85,000 + $12,750 + $25,600 = $663,650. Net proceeds out are $750,000 − $16,500 − $1,500 − $3,000 = $729,000. Net profit before tax is $65,350, an ROI of 9.8%, which annualises to 14.8% over the eight-month hold.

Because the property was held under 12 months, the entire gain would be added to your taxable income. At a 37% marginal rate the after-tax profit could fall to around $41,000.

Tax on a flip

Under ATO rules, profit on an investment property is generally a capital gain. If you hold the asset for less than 12 months, the whole gain is added to your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate. If you hold it for more than 12 months, individuals are generally entitled to the 50% CGT discount, which halves the taxable gain. Most flips are sold inside 12 months and miss the discount. The capital gains tax calculator works out the tax.

If the ATO considers that you are carrying on a property-flipping business or undertaking a profit-making scheme, your profit may be treated as ordinary income. The CGT discount then doesn't apply at all, and GST may also apply to the sale. Whether you are an investor or a trader is a matter of fact and intention. The main-residence exemption can apply if you live in the property, but moving in only to claim it while running flips as a business is the kind of arrangement the ATO scrutinises.

What this calculator leaves out

Capital gains tax, income tax and GST. Finance is only the monthly holding cost you enter, so loan establishment fees, lenders mortgage insurance (LMI, which typically applies above 80% LVR), break costs and capitalised interest need to go into your inputs if they apply. It assumes a single buy-and-sell cycle and doesn't handle subdivision, multi-dwelling development or rent earned during the hold. This page is general information, not personal financial advice.
