Last reviewed 12 July 2026. Tax, HECS and buffer figures are the FY2026-27 ones unless a line says otherwise.

How the figure is worked out

  1. Each income is taxed in full on the ATO resident individual scale for FY 2026-27 (the second rate dropped from 16% to 15% on 1 July 2026). The Medicare levy is 2% above $35,013 with a shading-in band from $28,011. Those are the FY 2025-26 low-income thresholds, the latest published, because each year's are legislated retrospectively.
  2. The after-tax amount is counted at a percentage for its type. PAYG salary counts at 100%, PAYG with bonus or commission at about 90%, casual, contract and self-employed income at about 80% (self-employed on a 2-year average) and rental income at about 75%. These percentages are my assumptions about common lender policy.
  3. Monthly commitments come off: living expenses (the higher of what you enter and the expense floor below), credit card limits at 3% per month, HECS at the marginal FY 2026-27 repayment rates, and any other debt repayments.
  4. What's left has to cover repayments worked out at your rate + 3.00%, the APRA serviceability buffer. The result is the largest loan that passes.

The 3% APRA serviceability buffer

Before October 2021, APRA required a 2.5% buffer above your loan rate. It lifted the buffer to 3.0% and has left it there since. On a loan priced at 6.25%, that 0.5% difference moves the test rate from 8.75% to 9.25%, which reduces borrowing capacity by about 4.4% in this calculator. With no buffer at all, the same surplus would support about 34% more loan.

The buffer is there so you can still afford repayments if rates rise. The cash rate went from 0.1% in late 2021 to 4.35% in late 2023.

The living-expense floor

Lenders use the higher of your declared expenses and HEM, the Melbourne Institute's quarterly benchmark of minimum living costs by household size and income. The full HEM table is paywalled commercial data that lenders subscribe to, and I don't have it. This calculator uses my own seven-tier estimate based on household income, adults and dependants, so a lender's floor may be higher or lower. If the living expenses you enter are above the floor, your figure is used.

Joint applications

Each applicant's income is taxed and counted separately, then added together. For a couple with one PAYG income of $100,000 and one casual income of $60,000, the PAYG applicant's $77,480 after tax counts in full and the casual applicant's after-tax income counts at 80%, which is $40,224. HECS is applied to each applicant who has it. The expense floor is for the household (two adults).

What the calculator leaves out

Negative gearing on existing investment properties, tax offsets, super contributions, foreign-currency income, and the add-backs lenders allow the self-employed (depreciation, interest, one-off costs). If any of those apply to you, a broker's figure will be closer than this one.
