How this asking price compares

Why a median is not a valuation
  • A median is the middle of all recorded sales or rents in the suburb over the period. It knows nothing about this property's bedrooms, land size, condition, street or aspect.
  • Government medians lag the market by a quarter or more, so a fast-moving suburb can have moved since the period shown.
  • The Victorian sale figures are preliminary 2025 full-year medians and may be revised by the Valuer-General.
  • A suburb with few sales in the period produces a noisy median. One expensive sale can move the figure.
Other checks on an asking price
  • Find the last 3–6 comparable sold prices (same suburb, similar bedrooms, land and condition). Sold prices tell you more than advertised guides.
  • Check days on market. A listing that has sat for months at the same price hasn't found a buyer at that price.
  • In Victoria, ask for the agent's Statement of Information. It must show three comparable sales and an indicative selling price.
  • Compare the implied gross rental yield with the suburb's rent levels. A very low yield means the price is high for the rent the property earns.

A suburb median is a general market indicator and doesn't value any individual property. This page is general information, not financial advice. Check figures against the credited government source, and check comparable sold prices, before making an offer.

Where the data comes from

All of it is open government data. I don't blend, model or extrapolate the figures. If a suburb or a state isn't in a free dataset I can lawfully republish, the tool says so.

  • Victoria: suburb-level house and unit sale medians from the Valuer-General's property sales statistics (2025 full year, preliminary, so final figures can differ). No free suburb rent medians.
  • South Australia: metro Adelaide house sale medians from the Valuer-General (Q1 2026) and median weekly rents from Consumer & Business Services (January–March 2026). Unit sale medians and non-metro suburbs aren't in the free release.
  • Queensland: median weekly rents from the Residential Tenancies Authority's bond data (March quarter 2026). There is no free suburb-level sale price dataset, so the checker runs a rent check and says so on screen.
  • Tasmania: the Department of Justice publishes raw rental-bond lodgements. I aggregate 12 months of them into suburb median rents and leave out suburbs with fewer than 10 bonds. No free suburb sale prices.
  • NSW, WA, the ACT and the NT: no suburb-level data I can republish yet. You can still enter an advertised rent for a yield check.

How the comparison works

Difference vs median (%) = (asking price − suburb median) ÷ suburb median

Within ±5% of the median counts as in line. 5–12% above is somewhat above, and more than 12% above is well above. 5–12% below is below, and more than 12% below is well below, which in auction states can signal underquoting.

Implied gross yield = (weekly rent × 52) ÷ asking price

The rent is the advertised rent if you enter one. Otherwise it is the suburb's median weekly rent where one exists. For a house in a suburb that also has a recorded house sale median, the checker works out the suburb's own reference yield (median rent against median price) and compares your listing to it. Where there's no local reference, it uses two rule-of-thumb bands: 2.5–4.5% gross for houses and 4–6% for units. Those bands are my own guide, not figures from a dataset. Gross yield ignores rates, insurance, maintenance, management and vacancy, so all yield output is labelled indicative.

Two worked examples

Point Cook, VIC (house). The Valuer-General's preliminary 2025 median house price for Point Cook is $787,500. A listing asking $830,000 is $830,000 − $787,500 = $42,500 above it, and $42,500 ÷ $787,500 = 5.4%. That lands just inside the "somewhat above the suburb median" band.

New Farm, QLD (house). Queensland publishes no free suburb sale prices, so the checker uses rent. The RTA's March 2026 median rent for New Farm is $700 a week, and $700 × 52 = $36,400 a year. Against a $1,300,000 asking price, the implied gross yield is $36,400 ÷ $1,300,000 = 2.8%. That is near the bottom of the 2.5–4.5% rule-of-thumb band this tool uses for houses.

What this checker doesn't cover

It doesn't value the specific property. It knows nothing about the land size, build quality, renovation state, zoning, easements or strata health. It has no sale prices for NSW, WA, TAS, ACT or NT (no free lawful source) or for QLD (paywalled), and nothing on off-market sales or brand-new builds with no comparable resale history. Medians also lag the market by at least a quarter. This page is general information, not personal financial advice or a valuation.
