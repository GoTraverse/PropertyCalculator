Land Tax Calculator

Works out annual land tax on investment land in each state and territory. It adds your properties together the way each state does, and applies the rules for companies, trusts and foreign owners.

Where and how you own

Your taxable land holdings

Enter taxable land values: the unimproved or site value on your valuation notice, not what the property would sell for. Leave out your own home (exempt everywhere) and primary production land.

Your estimated annual land tax

I checked the rate schedules against each state and territory revenue office on 5 July 2026, and the rates, thresholds and foreign owner surcharges for NSW, Victoria, Queensland, SA, WA, Tasmania and the ACT again on 18 November 2026.

How the calculator adds it up

Except in the ACT, land tax isn't worked out property by property. Each state adds up the taxable land value of everything you own inside its borders and applies its scale to the total. The scales start with a tax-free threshold and the marginal rates climb from there, so a second or third property in the same state can cost far more than the first.

South Australia adds your land together and then apportions the tax back across each property. The calculator shows the total only. The ACT doesn't aggregate at all: each property is assessed on its own 5-year-average AUV with its own fixed charge.

Worked example

A resident individual in Queensland owns investment properties, each on land valued at $400,000. With one, the land is under the $600,000 threshold and there's no land tax. With two, the aggregate is $800,000 and the bill is $500 plus 1 cent for each of the 200,000 dollars above the threshold, or $2,500 a year. With three, the $1.2 million aggregate reaches the next band: $4,500 plus 1.65 cents per dollar above $1 million, or $7,800 a year. The third property added $5,300 of tax, more than double what the second one did.

Bought in three different states, the same three blocks could pay nothing at all, because each state only counts the land inside its borders against its own threshold.

Assessment dates and thresholds

Land tax falls on whoever owns the land at one moment each year. If you sell the day after, you're still liable for the whole year in most states, and any adjustment between buyer and seller is a matter for the contract, not the revenue office.

What it leaves out

It covers each state's published scales and the main owner types. It doesn't check exemption conditions: the principal place of residence exemption has residency and use tests, and the primary production, boarding house, retirement village, charitable and build-to-rent concessions all have conditions of their own. It assumes a single owner. NSW and Queensland assess joint owners together first and then give each owner a deduction against their own assessment, which the calculator doesn't reproduce.

Victoria's vacant residential land tax and short-stay levy, and the Commonwealth's annual vacancy fee for some foreign owners, sit on top of land tax and aren't included. It also doesn't cover objecting to the land valuation itself.
