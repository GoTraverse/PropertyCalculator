Land transfer duty rates in Victoria

State Revenue Office Victoria (SRO) charges land transfer duty under the Duties Act 2000 on the dutiable value, which is normally the contract price. The bands are marginal up to $960,000. Between $960,000 and $2 million the duty is a flat 5.5% of the whole price, and above $2 million it’s $110,000 plus 6.5% of the amount over $2 million.

First home buyers in Victoria

Eligible first home buyers pay no land transfer duty on a property valued up to $600,000, and a reduced amount on a sliding scale from $600,000 to $750,000. Above $750,000 they pay standard rates. The exemption covers new and established homes used as a principal place of residence.

To qualify you must be 18 or older, never have received a first home buyer benefit anywhere in Australia, meet the citizenship or residency rules, and live in the property as your principal place of residence for at least 12 months, starting within 12 months of settlement. The full rules are on SRO Victoria’s first home buyer exemption page.

What this calculator leaves out

It doesn’t include the SRO’s principal place of residence (PPR) concession, which some owner-occupiers get separately from the first home buyer exemption. The result uses standard rates, which investors also pay, so treat it as the duty before any PPR concession and check your eligibility and the current value limits with the SRO.

First home buyer relief on vacant land has different rules and isn’t modelled, so land shows standard duty. Off-the-plan, pensioner and other concessions aren’t modelled either.

Frequently asked questions

When is stamp duty due in Victoria?

Within 30 days of settlement. In practice your conveyancer pays the SRO at settlement from your loan and deposit. Late payment attracts interest at the SRO’s published rate, and penalty tax in some cases.

What is the foreign purchaser additional duty in Victoria?

Foreign purchasers pay an extra 8% duty on top of standard land transfer duty when they buy residential property in Victoria, and the SRO collects it with the standard duty. Australian citizens and permanent residents aren’t foreign purchasers, and nor are New Zealand citizens on a Special Category Visa who meet the residency test.
