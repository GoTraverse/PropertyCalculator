How gross and net yield are worked out

  • Gross yield = (weekly rent × 52) ÷ property value × 100
  • Effective annual rent = weekly rent × (52 − vacancy weeks)
  • Annual expenses = council rates + landlord insurance + maintenance + (property management % × effective annual rent)
  • Net yield = (effective annual rent − annual expenses) ÷ property value × 100

Weekly rent is multiplied by 52 to get a year (some lenders use 52.14). The management fee is charged on rent collected, so the tool applies it to the effective rent after vacancy. Gross yield is the figure you can work out from a listing. It ignores every cost.

Worked example

A property worth $750,000 renting for $550 per week, with 2 weeks vacant a year, council rates of $2,200, landlord insurance of $1,500, maintenance of $2,500 and property management at 8% of rent collected.

  • Gross annual rent: $550 × 52 = $28,600
  • Gross yield: $28,600 ÷ $750,000 × 100 = 3.81%
  • Effective rent after 2 weeks vacant: $550 × 50 = $27,500
  • Management fee: 8% × $27,500 = $2,200
  • Total annual expenses: $2,200 + $1,500 + $2,500 + $2,200 = $8,400
  • Net income: $27,500 − $8,400 = $19,100
  • Net yield: $19,100 ÷ $750,000 × 100 = 2.55%

That 2.55% is before any loan interest.

What to enter for expenses

Use the real bills if you have them. If you don't, these are rough starting ranges with no dataset behind them:

  • Council rates: $1,500–$3,000/year, depending on the local council and land value
  • Water rates and charges: $800–$1,500/year (in some states tenants pay usage and owners pay supply)
  • Landlord insurance: $1,000–$2,500/year
  • Property management: 7–10% of rent, plus letting and admin fees
  • Maintenance and repairs: 0.5–1% of the property's value each year
  • Strata or body corporate fees on units and townhouses: can run into thousands

The form has no separate field for water or strata. Add them to the maintenance figure.

What this calculator leaves out

It stops at yield. Loan interest (the biggest holding cost for most investors), tax, depreciation, buying and selling costs and capital growth are all outside it. The property cash flow calculator adds the loan and the tax. This is general information, not personal financial advice.
