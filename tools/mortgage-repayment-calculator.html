The formula

monthly = P × (r/12) ÷ (1 − (1 + r/12)−n)   where P is the loan amount, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, and n is the number of monthly periods (term × 12).

Each repayment covers the interest on the outstanding balance plus some principal. Early in the loan most of it is interest. Total interest over a 30-year loan at 6.25% on $600,000 is about $730,000, more than the original loan.

Fortnightly and weekly repayments

The fortnightly figure is half the monthly repayment, paid every 14 days. There are 26 fortnights in a year, so that adds up to 13 monthly payments a year instead of 12. On a $600,000 30-year 6.25% mortgage, this calculator shows that switching from monthly to half-monthly fortnightly repayments pays the loan off 5 years 8 months earlier and saves $162,171 in interest. The figure comes from simulating the fortnightly schedule.

The weekly figure is a quarter of the monthly repayment. Paid 52 times a year that is the same annual total as the fortnightly schedule, so the saving is about the same, with weekly a little ahead because each payment lands earlier.

Offset

Interest is calculated each day on (loan balance − offset balance). The scheduled repayment doesn't change, but less of it goes to interest, so the loan is paid off sooner. Keeping $20,000 in an offset on a $500,000 30-year 6.25% loan saves $97,632 in interest over the life of the loan in this calculator and pays it off 2 years 7 months earlier.

The LMI estimate

If your loan-to-value ratio (LVR) is over 80% you'll usually pay lenders mortgage insurance, a one-off premium that protects the lender if you default. It doesn't protect you. It can be added to the loan, and then you pay interest on it too. This calculator adds it to the total cost but not to the loan, so the repayments above leave it out.
