How the total is worked out

  • Loan amount = purchase price − (purchase price × deposit %).
  • LVR = loan amount ÷ purchase price. LMI is added when the deposit is under 20%, and the rate rises with the LVR.
  • Stamp duty = the state's tiered rate on the price, less the first home buyer concession if you tick that box, plus the foreign buyer surcharge if you tick that one. Queensland also charges a lower home concession rate to a buyer who will live in the home, so a "Buying to live in" box appears when you pick Queensland and haven't ticked first home buyer. No other state's duty depends on it. The Queensland stamp duty calculator lists the conditions for the home concession.
  • Conveyancing is a flat $1,800 in every state. That's my estimate, and I'd expect quotes between $1,300 and $2,200.
  • Total buying costs = stamp duty + registration fees + conveyancing + lender fees (valuation, loan application, settlement) + building and pest inspection + first-year building and contents insurance + LMI + moving costs + any lease-break cost.
  • Cash needed = deposit + total buying costs.

What the first home buyer box does in each state

The calculator assumes an established home. Ticking first home buyer gives a full exemption up to one price and a sliding concession up to a higher one in NSW, Victoria and WA, with full duty above that, and applies Queensland's first home concession. In the ACT, the Home Buyer Concession Scheme charges eligible buyers no duty, with no income test or price cap from 1 July 2026. South Australia's relief covers new homes and vacant land only, and the NT's covers house-and-land packages, so the calculator charges full duty in both. Tasmania's established-home exemption ended 30 June 2026. The stamp duty calculator lists each state's thresholds, and the first home buyer grants calculator covers the grant and the federal schemes.

Ticking foreign buyer adds the state's surcharge on top of standard duty. The calculator has no surcharge for the ACT or the NT.

Worked example

A buyer who isn't a first home buyer pays $650,000 for an established home in New South Wales with a 10% deposit ($65,000). The loan is $585,000 and the LVR is 90%. The calculator gives:

  • Stamp duty (transfer duty): $23,438
  • Title office registration fees: $366
  • Conveyancing and legal: $1,800
  • Lender fees: bank valuation $650, loan application fee $1,170, settlement fee $250
  • Building and pest inspection: $500
  • First-year insurance: building $2,600, contents $500
  • LMI at 90% LVR (1.9% of the loan): $11,115
  • Moving costs: $2,500

Total buying costs are $44,889, or 6.9% of the price. With the $65,000 deposit, the cash needed is $109,889.

An eligible first home buyer would pay no stamp duty on this purchase in NSW, which removes the $23,438. LMI still shows when the first home buyer box is ticked. The 5% Deposit Scheme removes it if you and the property qualify, and the calculator doesn't check that.

What the calculator leaves out

  • Council and water rate adjustments at settlement, and utility connection fees.
  • Strata or body corporate levies, council rates, land tax and insurance renewals after the first year.
  • Landlord insurance if you're buying to rent out.
  • Loan repayments, and capital gains tax when you sell.
  • Scheme eligibility, and anything specific to your contract or lender.

For a settlement figure, get a quote from your conveyancer and a duty assessment from your state revenue office.
