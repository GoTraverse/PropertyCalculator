How the duty is worked out

Each state and territory sets its own duty, so the same $650,000 purchase costs a different amount on each side of a border. Most schedules are brackets: a fixed amount for the bracket the price falls in, plus a marginal rate on the part of the price above that bracket's lower threshold. Duty is charged on the price or the market value, whichever is higher, which matters for gifts and sales between relatives.

The first home buyer box applies the state's exemption or concession, and the foreign buyer box adds its surcharge (a flat percentage of the whole price, on top of standard duty). To compare states I'd use the effective rate (total duty divided by the price), because a "5.5% top bracket" only applies to the slice of the price inside that bracket.

State by state

New South Wales (transfer duty)

The brackets rise to 5.5%, with a premium rate on very expensive homes. First home buyers pay nothing up to $800,000 and get a reducing concession above that. Foreign buyers pay a 9% surcharge.

Victoria (land transfer duty)

The brackets rise to 6.5%. First home buyers pay nothing up to $600,000, with a concession above that. The First Home Owner Grant for new builds is a separate payment. Foreign purchasers pay additional duty.

Queensland (transfer duty)

Owner-occupiers get a lower "home" rate than investors, which is why the buyer profile field changes the answer here and in no other state. First home buyers pay nothing up to $700,000 (and on new homes at any price), with a concession above that. Foreign buyers pay additional foreign acquirer duty (AFAD). There is also a Queensland-only stamp duty calculator.

South Australia (stamp duty)

There is no first home relief on an established home. First home buyers pay no duty on eligible new homes and vacant land. A foreign ownership surcharge applies.

Western Australia (transfer duty)

WA uses one general rate scale. First home buyers pay nothing up to $600,000 on transactions from 7 May 2026, with a concession above that. Foreign buyers pay a 7% surcharge.

Tasmania (property transfer duty)

The first home buyer exemption on established homes up to $750,000 expired on 30 June 2026 and was not extended, so a purchase settling after that date pays full duty. Pensioner concessions exist within value limits. Foreign buyers pay an 8% Foreign Investor Duty Surcharge.

Australian Capital Territory (conveyance duty)

From 1 July 2026 the Home Buyer Concession Scheme has no income test and no property value limit. An eligible buyer (no property owned in the previous five years, at least one year living in the home) pays no duty at any price. The ACT is also phasing conveyance duty down as part of a long-term tax reform.

Northern Territory (stamp duty)

Duty is a formula below $525,000 and a flat percentage of the whole price above it. Relief is limited to house-and-land packages, with nothing for first home buyers of established homes. The NT has no foreign buyer surcharge.

Worked example

A $650,000 established home in New South Wales, bought by an owner-occupier who is not a first home buyer, with a 20% deposit ($130,000) and a $520,000 loan. Under the FY 2026-27 brackets the transfer duty is $23,438, an effective rate of 3.61%, and the property plus duty comes to $673,438.

An eligible first home buyer pays $0 at the same price, because $650,000 is under the NSW exemption threshold. A foreign buyer also pays the 9% surcharge ($58,500), so the total is $81,938.

Who counts as a first home buyer or a foreign buyer

First home buyer relief usually requires that you (and often your partner) have never owned residential property in Australia, that you move in within a set period and that you stay for a minimum time. A former investment property can rule you out even if you never lived in it.

The foreign surcharge turns on residency. Temporary visa holders can be caught by it, and so can foreign companies and trusts.

What the calculator leaves out

It works from the price alone, so it doesn't cover pensioner concessions, off-the-plan reductions in dutiable value, deceased-estate and family-transfer exemptions, or primary-production land. Every dwelling is treated as an established home, which means first home relief that depends on the home being new (as in Queensland and South Australia) isn't applied. The First Home Owner Grant is a separate cash grant and isn't included. Land tax and council rates aren't either.

Conveyancing is a flat $1,800 in every state. That's my estimate, and I'd expect quotes between $1,300 and $2,200. The LMI figure is my own estimate by LVR band, with no published rate table behind it, so ask your lender for the premium. Get the duty assessed by your state revenue office and a quote from your conveyancer or solicitor before settlement. This is general information, not financial or tax advice.
