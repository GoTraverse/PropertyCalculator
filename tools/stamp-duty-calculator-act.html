ACT conveyance duty rates

The ACT Revenue Office charges conveyance duty under the Duties Act 1999. Rates step up across six brackets, from 0.28% on the first $260,000 to 6.4% on the part between $1,000,000 and $1,455,000. Above $1,455,000 the brackets stop and duty is a flat 4.54% of the whole dutiable value.

Home Buyer Concession Scheme

Under the Home Buyer Concession Scheme (HBCS) an eligible buyer pays no conveyance duty at any price. It isn't limited to first home buyers. To qualify, you can't have owned property in the previous five years, and you have to live in the home for at least one year. From 1 July 2026 the property value cap (about $1,020,000), the household income test (about $250,000) and the maximum concession amount were all abolished. The HBCS replaced the First Home Owner Grant and the earlier duty concession in 2019.

Investors pay the standard rates in the table, with no concession, and so does an owner-occupier who doesn't qualify for the HBCS. The rules and the application are on the ACT Revenue Office's HBCS page.

What the calculator leaves out

It doesn't model the concession for vacant land, so for land it shows standard duty even if you tick the HBCS box. Nor does it include off-the-plan, pensioner or other concessions.

Frequently asked questions

When is stamp duty due in the ACT?

After settlement. Your conveyancer has 14 days after settlement to lodge the transfer with Access Canberra. Once the title is registered, the ACT Revenue Office emails a notice of assessment and the duty is due 14 days after registration, paid by BPAY or bank transfer. It can't be paid before settlement or when the transfer is lodged. Late payment attracts interest at the rate the ACT Revenue Office publishes.

Does the ACT charge a foreign buyer surcharge?

Not on duty. Instead, foreign owners of residential property pay an annual land tax surcharge of 0.75% of the property's average unimproved value, billed with land tax for as long as they own it. Australian citizens and permanent residents don't pay it.

Is ACT stamp duty being abolished?

The ACT has cut residential conveyance duty rates every year for over a decade as part of a long-running tax reform, and has gradually raised general rates (council rates) on residential property to make up for it. There's no firm date for abolishing duty altogether.
