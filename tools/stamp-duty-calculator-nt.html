NT stamp duty rates

The Territory Revenue Office charges stamp duty on conveyances under the Stamp Duty Act 1978. It's worked out on the dutiable value, usually the contract price. Below $525,000 the NT uses a formula instead of brackets, so the rate rises smoothly with the price. From $525,000 up, one flat rate applies to the whole price: 4.95% up to $3 million, 5.75% up to $5 million and 5.95% above that.

Dutiable valueStandard rate
$0 to $525,000D = (0.06571441 × V²) + 15V (V in thousands)
$525,001 to $3,000,0004.95% of dutiable value
$3,000,001 to $5,000,0005.75% of dutiable value
Above $5,000,0005.95% of dutiable value

In the formula, D is the duty in dollars and V is the dutiable value in thousands of dollars.

No concession on an established home

On an established home, owner-occupiers and first home buyers pay the same standard rates as investors. Separate home buyer cash grants may apply, but they don't reduce duty.

House and Land Package Exemption

A house and land package is fully exempt from stamp duty for contracts entered into between 1 July 2022 and 30 June 2027. There's no cap on the property value, and you don't have to be a first home buyer. Your conveyancer lodges the exemption claim. The rules are on the NT Government's stamp duty page.

What the calculator leaves out

There's no option for a house and land package, so it shows full duty. If your contract qualifies for the exemption, you pay none. Other concessions, such as off-the-plan or pensioner relief, aren't modelled.

Frequently asked questions

When is stamp duty due in the NT?

NT stamp duty is payable within 60 days of the dutiable transaction. Your conveyancer normally pays the Territory Revenue Office at settlement out of your loan and deposit. Late payment attracts interest and penalty tax under the Taxation Administration Act (NT).

Does the NT charge a foreign buyer surcharge?

No. The Northern Territory doesn't levy a foreign buyer surcharge on residential property, so a foreign buyer pays the standard rates. The NT and the ACT are the only Australian jurisdictions with no foreign purchaser duty surcharge.
