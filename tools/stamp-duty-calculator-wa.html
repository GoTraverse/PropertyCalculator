Transfer duty rates in Western Australia

RevenueWA charges transfer duty under the Duties Act 2008. The dutiable value is normally the purchase price, but RevenueWA can substitute a higher market valuation. Owner-occupiers and investors pay the same general (residential) rate, and the First Home Owner Rate of Duty is the only relief for owner-occupiers.

First home buyers in Western Australia

Under the First Home Owner Rate of Duty (FHOR), eligible first home buyers pay no transfer duty on a home up to $600,000, and a reduced amount on a sliding scale from $600,000 to $800,000. Above $800,000 they pay the general rate. For vacant land the exemption applies up to $450,000, with a concession to $550,000.

The 2026-27 Budget announced the rise from $500,000 and $700,000, applying retrospectively to transactions entered into from 7 May 2026. Confirm the current legislative status with RevenueWA.

To qualify you must be eligible for the First Home Owner Grant (citizenship, first home, and living in it as your principal residence), and your settlement agent lodges the FHOR claim. The First Home Owner Grant’s property value cap south of the 26th parallel is $800,000. RevenueWA’s First Home Owner Rate fact sheet has the full rules.

What this calculator leaves out

It doesn’t model the first home owner rate for vacant land, so land shows standard duty. Off-the-plan, pensioner and other concessions aren’t modelled either.

Frequently asked questions

When is WA transfer duty due?

Generally within two months of the dutiable transaction, which usually means two months from the contract date. Your settlement agent normally pays RevenueWA at settlement. Late payment attracts penalty tax and interest at RevenueWA’s published rate.

How does WA’s 7% foreign buyers duty compare with other states?

WA introduced its 7% foreign buyers duty in 2019. It’s charged on top of standard transfer duty whenever a foreign person buys residential property. On the schedules these calculators use, that rate matches SA and is below the 8% in VIC, QLD and TAS and the 9% in NSW. The ACT and NT have no duty surcharge.

Is “stamp duty” the same as “transfer duty” in WA?

Yes. The Duties Act 2008 calls it transfer duty, and so do RevenueWA’s forms and settlement statements. Most people still call it stamp duty.
