How the tax is worked out

CGT has no rate of its own. Your net capital gain is added to your assessable income in the financial year the contract of sale is signed, and taxed at your marginal income tax rate. The calculator follows these steps:

  • Cost base = purchase price + buying and selling costs (stamp duty, conveyancing, agent commission, legal fees) + capital improvements (renovations, extensions, a new kitchen).
  • Gross capital gain = sale price − cost base.
  • Discounted gain = gross gain × 50%, only if you are an individual and held the asset for more than 12 months.
  • Tax payable = (tax on [your other taxable income + discounted gain]) − (tax on your other taxable income alone), using the ATO's resident marginal rates.

Any part of the gain that pushes you into a higher bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate, so the same gain costs less tax in a year when your other income is low. The figure includes the 2% Medicare levy on the gain.

The cost base can also include some ownership costs that were never claimed as a deduction. You need records for anything you add to it.

Worked example

An individual Australian resident sells an investment property for $900,000. They paid $600,000, spent $45,000 on buying and selling costs (purchase stamp duty, conveyancing, and agent commission on sale) and $0 on capital improvements, held it for more than 12 months, and have $100,000 of other taxable income that year.

  • Cost base: $600,000 + $45,000 = $645,000
  • Gross capital gain: $900,000 − $645,000 = $255,000
  • Discounted gain: $127,500, which lifts taxable income for the year to $227,500
  • On the FY 2026-27 scale, including the Medicare levy, tax on $227,500 is $72,795 and tax on $100,000 is $22,520, so the CGT is $50,275
  • Net gain after tax: $204,725

Dates that change the result

The CGT event is the day you sign the contract of sale, not settlement. A late-June contract falls in a different financial year to an early-July one, which can change your marginal rate.

The discount needs ownership for longer than 12 months, counted from the day after you acquired the property. Selling before then loses the whole 50% discount.

The main residence exemption

Your home is generally exempt from CGT. Broadly, the dwelling must have been your home, you must not have used it to produce income beyond limited circumstances, and it must sit on land of two hectares or less. Under the six-year rule, if you move out and rent the property it can remain CGT-exempt for up to six years, provided you don't treat another property as your main residence at the same time. Ticking the main residence box sets the CGT to $0. The calculator can't check these conditions against your facts.

The 50% discount is replaced from 1 July 2027

The capital gains tax reforms announced in the 2026-27 Federal Budget are now law (Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026). From 1 July 2027, the 50% CGT discount for individuals, trusts and partnerships is replaced by cost-base indexation plus a 30% minimum tax rate on gains that accrue after that date. Gains accrued before it keep the current treatment, so a property held across the date is split between the old and new rules. The ATO's new-legislation guidance explains the split.

This calculator applies FY2026-27 law, with the full 50% discount where the 12-month rule is met.

What this calculator leaves out

It covers one property sold by an individual Australian resident, and includes the 2% Medicare levy with its low-income phase-in. It doesn't handle ownership through a company, trust or self-managed super fund (companies get no 50% discount, and complying super funds get a partial one). Nor does it handle a non-resident seller. Non-residents generally can't claim the discount on gains accrued while a foreign resident, and the main residence exemption is largely unavailable to them.

It also leaves out the Medicare levy surcharge, tax offsets such as the low income tax offset, capital losses carried forward from prior years, the partial exemption where a former home was rented out, depreciation and capital works (Division 43) adjustments that can reduce the cost base, and first-home or small-business concessions.

This page is general information, not personal tax advice. The current rules are on the ATO's capital gains tax pages.
