Assumptions

Tax uses FY2026-27 Australian resident rates ($0–$18,200 at 0%, to $45,000 at 15%, to $135,000 at 30%, to $190,000 at 37%, 45% above). It is worked out in full with and without the property, so a loss that crosses a bracket is handled correctly.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income, phased in for low incomes: nil up to $28,011, then 10c per $1 over $28,011 until it reaches the full 2% at $35,013 (single thresholds for FY2025-26, the latest published). The family and seniors thresholds, tax offsets and the Medicare levy surcharge are ignored.

On P&I loans, year-1 deductible interest is approximated as loan × rate. The deductible share falls slowly as principal is repaid; the cash repayment figure is exact.

Models one full year at a fixed rate and rent. Leaves out CGT on sale, LMI, borrowing-cost amortisation, and ownership through trusts or companies.

General information, not tax or financial advice. What you can deduct depends on your circumstances, so check with a registered tax agent or the ATO.

How it works

The calculator keeps two ledgers, because your bank account and the ATO count different things.

Pre-tax cash flow = rent collected (52 weeks less your vacancy allowance) − cash expenses (management, rates, water, insurance, repairs, strata, land tax, other) − the loan payment. On an interest-only loan the payment is loan × rate. On a principal and interest loan it's the full repayment, principal included, because that money leaves your account even though the ATO doesn't treat it as an expense.

Taxable result = rent − (cash expenses + loan interest + depreciation). Only the interest is deductible, never the principal, and depreciation reduces taxable income without costing any cash this year. A negative result means the property is negatively geared, and the loss reduces the tax on your other income.

Your tax is worked out twice on the full FY2026-27 resident scale, once on your income alone and once with the property's result included. The difference is the tax benefit (or extra tax). Because both bills are worked out in full, the answer is right even when the loss takes you across a bracket, which a single "loss × marginal rate" shortcut gets wrong.

After-tax cash flow = pre-tax cash flow + the tax benefit (or minus the extra tax, if the property makes a profit). Divided by 52, it's the weekly figure at the top of the result.

Worked example

A $650,000 unit with a $520,000 interest-only loan at 5.5%. Rent is $570 a week with 2 weeks vacancy, which gives $28,500 of annual rent. Management at 7% costs $1,995. Add rates $2,000, water $1,200, insurance $1,800 and maintenance $1,500 for total cash expenses of $8,495. Interest at 5.5% on $520,000 is $28,600. Cash out is $37,095 against $28,500 in, a pre-tax cash flow of −$8,595, about −$165 a week.

On the tax side, with an $8,000 depreciation schedule, deductions total $8,495 + $28,600 + $8,000 = $45,095, so the taxable result is −$16,595. For an investor on $95,000, tax (including the 2% Medicare levy) falls from about $20,920 to about $15,610. That's a benefit of $5,310. After-tax cash flow is −$8,595 + $5,310 = −$3,285 a year, roughly −$63 a week.

The FY2026-27 tax rates used

The calculator uses the Australian resident rates that apply from 1 July 2026:

  • $0 to $18,200: nil
  • $18,201 to $45,000: 15c per $1 over $18,200
  • $45,001 to $135,000: 30c per $1 over $45,000
  • $135,001 to $190,000: 37c per $1 over $135,000
  • $190,001 and over: 45c per $1 over $190,000

The 15% rate is new this financial year (it was 16% in FY2025-26) and is legislated to fall to 14% from 1 July 2027, which will slightly reduce the value of deductions in that band. The Medicare levy is added on top. It's nil on a taxable income up to $28,011, then 10c per $1 over $28,011 until that reaches 2% of your income at $35,013, and 2% of taxable income above that. Those are the single thresholds for FY2025-26, the latest published. The family and seniors thresholds, tax offsets and the Medicare levy surcharge aren't modelled, and they can move your bill either way.

Depreciation

Division 43 capital works deductions let you claim 2.5% of the original construction cost a year for 40 years on residential buildings constructed after mid-September 1987. Division 40 plant and equipment covers items such as ovens, carpets, blinds and air-conditioners, each depreciated over its effective life. Since 9 May 2017, buyers of established homes generally can't claim Division 40 deductions on second-hand items that came with the property, only on items they buy new. New builds aren't affected. Enter the yearly figure from a quantity surveyor's schedule in the depreciation field. It lowers your taxable result without changing your cash flow.

Negative gearing changes from 1 July 2027

The negative gearing reform announced in the 2026-27 Federal Budget is now law. From 1 July 2027, negative gearing deductions for residential property are limited to new builds. Properties held at 7:30pm AEST on 12 May 2026 are grandfathered, so existing holdings keep negative gearing. An established home bought after that cut-off can't be negatively geared from 1 July 2027, which means its rental losses will no longer reduce the tax on your other income.

The calculator's tax benefit is correct for every property in FY2026-27, and after that for grandfathered holdings and new builds. The sale changes too. For gains that accrue after 1 July 2027, the 50% CGT discount is replaced by cost-base indexation plus a 30% minimum tax rate (gains accrued before then keep the current treatment). If you're weighing up an established home now, check that it still works without the gearing refund. Source: ATO new-legislation guidance on the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026, checked 5 July 2026.

What it leaves out

  • Capital gains tax when you sell, which the capital gains tax calculator covers.
  • LMI, loan establishment fees and other borrowing costs over $100. These are deducted over five years (or the loan term if shorter), not in year one.
  • A PAYG withholding variation, which lets you reduce the tax taken from your salary during the year instead of waiting until July. The yearly total is the same.
  • The family and seniors Medicare levy thresholds, the levy surcharge and tax offsets. The single low-income thresholds are used.
  • Ownership through a trust or company. Company profits are taxed at company rates, and trust losses generally stay in the trust instead of reducing the tax on your salary. The calculator models individual ownership only.
  • Later years. Rents rise, rates move and the interest share of a P&I repayment falls each year, so re-run it when your numbers change.
