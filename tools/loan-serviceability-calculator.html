How lenders work it out, and what the calculator does

Australian lenders don't lend a fixed multiple of your salary.

  1. Income. Lenders count only part of overtime, bonuses, commission and rent. The calculator counts salary at 100% and other income at 75%.
  2. Tax. Income tax and the Medicare levy come off. The calculator uses the FY 2026-27 resident scale.
  3. Living expenses. Lenders use the higher of your declared spending and the Household Expenditure Measure (HEM) for your household size and income, a benchmark published quarterly by the Melbourne Institute. The HEM table is licensed data I don't have, so the calculator uses my own rough floor instead, based on household income and one or two adults, with no dependants.
  4. Existing debts. Repayments on car loans, personal loans, buy-now-pay-later and HECS/HELP come off, plus a notional repayment on the full limit of every credit card, used or not. The calculator takes the repayments you enter and 3% of your total card limits a month.
  5. The buffer. The new loan is tested at its rate plus APRA's serviceability buffer of 3 percentage points. The calculator tests a 30-year principal-and-interest loan at your rate plus 3%, and the estimate is the loan your monthly surplus would repay at that rate.

Lenders also look at debt-to-income (total debt against gross income). The calculator doesn't apply a debt-to-income cap.

Worked example

A couple apply together. One earns $120,000 and the other $85,000, with no other income. They have a car loan costing $650 a month and one credit card with a $10,000 limit, and they enter living expenses of $4,200 a month and a loan rate of 6.0%. The calculator gives:

  • Net income counted: $13,197 a month, after income tax and the Medicare levy of $28,920 and $17,720 a year.
  • Living expenses used: $4,590 a month. The floor for two adults on $205,000 is higher than the $4,200 entered, so the floor is used.
  • Existing commitments: $650 for the car loan, plus $300 for the credit card (3% of the $10,000 limit), even if the card is paid off in full each month.
  • Test rate: 6.0% plus the 3% buffer, so 9.0%.
  • Surplus for repayments: $13,197 − $4,590 − $650 − $300 = $7,657 a month.
  • Estimated borrowing capacity: $951,585, the loan that $7,657 a month repays at 9.0% over 30 years. At the actual 6.0% rate the repayment on that loan would be about $5,705 a month.

With a $20,000 card limit instead, the estimate falls to $914,300. With a $5,000 limit it rises to $970,227.

What the calculator leaves out

It doesn't copy any lender's servicing model. Banks use different expense tables, floor rates and policies, so two lenders can give different answers for the same applicant. It also leaves out dependants, income shading on salary, HECS as a separate repayment, lenders mortgage insurance, loan-to-value limits, and stamp duty and other purchase costs (the cost of purchase calculator adds those up).
