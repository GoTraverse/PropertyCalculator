How the total is worked out

The duty saving is measured against what an owner-occupier who isn't a first home buyer would pay, which in Queensland is the lower home concession rate. LMI avoided is my own estimate, 3.4% of a 95% loan, with no published rate table behind it. It only counts if the price is within the 5% Deposit Scheme cap. The FHOG is a state cash grant of $10,000 to $50,000 depending on the state, and only for new homes. Help to Buy and the First Home Super Saver are shown in the result but not added to the total.

From 1 August 2026, Queensland's first home duty concessions only go to Australian citizens, permanent residents and specified foreign retirees. WA's new thresholds were announced in the 2026-27 Budget and apply back to transactions from 7 May 2026, so check their legislative status with RevenueWA. The ACT's Home Buyer Concession Scheme needs you to have owned no property in the previous 5 years and to live in the home for 12 months.

5% Deposit Scheme

The 5% Deposit Scheme, formerly the First Home Guarantee, is run by Housing Australia through a panel of participating lenders. You can buy with a 5% deposit and pay no LMI, because the government guarantees the lender against the shortfall. On 1 October 2025 the $125,000 and $200,000 income caps and the limit on places were removed. The regional and family guarantees were folded in at the same time, so regional buyers and single parents now apply through this scheme.

The rules as at July 2026:

  • You're a first home buyer, or you haven't owned property in Australia in the last 10 years.
  • You'll live in the home.
  • You're an Australian citizen or permanent resident, aged 18 or over.
  • The price is within the cap for the area. Examples: $1.5m in Sydney and NSW regional centres ($800k in the rest of NSW), $1m in Brisbane, the Gold Coast and the Sunshine Coast ($700k in the rest of QLD), $950k in Melbourne and $850k in Perth.

Help to Buy

Help to Buy is the federal shared-equity scheme. Applications opened on 5 December 2025 through Commonwealth Bank and Bank Australia, with more lenders joining during 2026. The government takes a stake of up to 40% in a new home or 30% in an established one, and your deposit can be as little as 2%. On a $600,000 new home that's $240,000 of government equity, a $12,000 deposit and a $348,000 loan (58% of the price). When you sell or refinance, the government takes its proportional share of any capital gain.

Income caps from 1 July 2026 are $103,000 single and $165,000 for joint applicants or single parents, and there are 10,000 places in FY2026-27. Help to Buy has its own price caps, which differ from the 5% Deposit Scheme's: $1.3m in Sydney against $1.5m, for example, and $600k flat in the NT. You can't use it together with the 5% Deposit Scheme.

First Home Super Saver

You can put voluntary contributions of up to $15,000 a year into super, to a total of $50,000 per person ($100,000 for a couple), and later withdraw them for a first home deposit along with deemed earnings (roughly the 90-day Bank Bill Swap rate plus 3%). Pre-tax contributions are taxed at 15% inside super instead of at your marginal rate (32-47%). The amount released is taxed at your marginal rate less a 30% offset. It suits people 3-5 years from buying. A withdrawal takes about 25 business days to process, so apply before you start house hunting.

What the calculator leaves out

  • Eligibility rules other than price, income (for Help to Buy) and property type. "Eligible" in the result means you pass those checks.
  • Federal price caps by exact postcode. The calculator only knows capital city or regional centre versus the rest of the state.
  • WA's higher FHOG cap north of the 26th parallel ($1,000,000). The southern $800,000 cap is used everywhere in WA.
  • Whether an NT new build is a house-and-land package. Off-the-plan apartments may not get the duty relief the calculator gives them.
  • Vacant land duty concessions, such as WA's $450k exemption and $550k concession thresholds.
  • State government home lenders and shared-equity schemes, such as Keystart in WA and HomeStart Finance in SA.
  • The Defence Home Ownership Assistance Scheme, which is only for Defence Force members.
