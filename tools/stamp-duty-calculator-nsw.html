NSW transfer duty rates

Revenue NSW collects transfer duty (most people still call it stamp duty) under the Duties Act 1997, on the dutiable value. That's usually the contract price, though Revenue NSW can substitute a higher market value if it thinks the price has been understated. The brackets are indexed to CPI on 1 July each year. No home concession

Unlike Victoria and Queensland, NSW gives owner-occupiers no concession, so they pay the same rates as investors. The only relief for someone buying a home is the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme below, or the First Home Owner Grant on a new build.

First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme

Under the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme (FHBAS) an eligible first home buyer pays no transfer duty up to $800,000, and a reduced amount between $800,000 and $1,000,000. Above $1,000,000 there's no concession. The thresholds are the same for new and established homes. Vacant land you'll build a home on is exempt up to $350,000, with a partial concession to $450,000.

To qualify you must be 18 or older, an Australian citizen or permanent resident, and never have owned residential property in Australia. You also have to move in within 12 months of settlement and live there for at least 12 continuous months. The rules and the application are on Revenue NSW's FHBAS page.

What the calculator leaves out

It doesn't apply the vacant land concession, so for land it shows standard duty even if you tick first home buyer. Between $800,000 and $1,000,000 it scales a first home buyer's duty in a straight line from nil up to the full standard amount. Off-the-plan and pensioner concessions aren't included either.

Frequently asked questions

When is stamp duty due in NSW?

NSW transfer duty is payable within three months of liability, which is usually the contract date. Your solicitor or conveyancer normally pays it at settlement. Late payment attracts interest and penalty tax under the Taxation Administration Act 1996 (NSW).

What is the NSW foreign buyer surcharge?

A foreign buyer of residential property pays a 9% surcharge purchaser duty on top of standard transfer duty, and Revenue NSW collects both at the same time. Australian citizens and permanent residents don't pay it. A New Zealand citizen on a Special Category Visa is treated as foreign for this surcharge unless they are also an Australian permanent resident.
