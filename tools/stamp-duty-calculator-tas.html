Property transfer duty rates in Tasmania

The State Revenue Office Tasmania (SRO Tas) charges property transfer duty under the Duties Act 2001 on the dutiable value, which is usually the contract price. Duty is a flat $50 up to $3,000. Above that, marginal rates rise to 4.5% on the value over $725,000, and the $50 base carries through every band. Owner-occupiers and investors pay the same rates.

First home buyers in Tasmania

Tasmania no longer has a first home buyer duty exemption. The temporary exemption for established homes valued up to $750,000 expired on 30 June 2026 and wasn’t extended, so a first home buyer settling after that date pays full standard duty. SRO Tasmania still has a page on the ended exemption.

If you’re building or buying a new home, you may be eligible for the First Home Owner Grant instead: $20,000 for eligible transactions from 1 July 2026 ($10,000 base plus a $10,000 additional grant, subject to the passage of legislation). The previous $30,000 grant closed on 30 June 2026. The grant is a cash payment and doesn’t reduce the duty.

What this calculator leaves out

The result doesn’t include the First Home Owner Grant or any off-the-plan, pensioner or other concession.

Frequently asked questions

When is stamp duty due in Tasmania?

Within three months of the dutiable transaction. Your conveyancer normally pays SRO Tas at settlement. Late payment attracts interest at the rate SRO Tas publishes.

Does Tasmania charge a foreign buyer surcharge?

Yes. Since 1 April 2020 a foreign person buying residential property in Tasmania pays the Foreign Investor Duty Surcharge (FIDS) of 8% of the dutiable value, on top of standard duty. Australian citizens and permanent residents aren’t foreign persons for this surcharge.
